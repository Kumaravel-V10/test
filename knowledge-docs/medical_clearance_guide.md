## 1. Overview

This document outlines the airline's medical clearance requirements for passengers with health conditions that may affect their fitness to fly. Medical clearance ensures passenger safety, the safety of other passengers, and compliance with aviation regulations. All customer service agents must be familiar with these procedures to correctly identify when medical clearance is required and how to process requests.

---

## 2. MEDIF Form Requirements

### 2.1 What is MEDIF?

The MEDIF (Medical Information Form) is the standardized IATA form used by airlines to assess a passenger's fitness to fly. It consists of two parts that must be completed and submitted to the airline's Medical Department for review.

### 2.2 MEDIF Part 1 — Passenger Information

Completed by the **passenger or their representative**. Contains:

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Full name | As per travel document |
| Date of birth | DD/MM/YYYY |
| Booking reference (PNR) | 6-character alphanumeric code |
| Flight details | Flight number(s), date(s), route(s) |
| Contact information | Phone number, email address |
| Nature of condition | Brief description of the medical condition |
| Mobility status | Can walk independently / needs wheelchair / immobile |
| Oxygen requirement | Yes/No. If yes, flow rate (liters per minute) |
| Stretcher requirement | Yes/No |
| Accompanying medical personnel | Yes/No. If yes, name and qualifications |
| Special equipment | List of medical equipment being carried |
| Emergency contact | Name, relationship, phone number |

### 2.3 MEDIF Part 2 — Physician's Assessment

Completed by the **passenger's treating physician**. Contains:

| Section | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Diagnosis** | Primary diagnosis and relevant secondary conditions |
| **Current condition** | Stable / Improving / Deteriorating |
| **Date of onset / surgery / hospitalization** | When the condition began or was treated |
| **Prognosis for travel** | Physician's assessment of fitness to fly |
| **Medications** | Current medications, dosages, and administration schedule |
| **Oxygen requirement** | Continuous / Intermittent. Flow rate in L/min. Duration of need. |
| **Cabin pressure tolerance** | Can the patient tolerate reduced cabin pressure (equivalent to ~6,000–8,000 ft altitude)? |
| **Risk of contagion** | Is the condition communicable? If yes, specify precautions. |
| **Mental state** | Is the patient cooperative and able to follow safety instructions? |
| **Need for medical escort** | Is a qualified medical escort required? |
| **Special seating** | Any seating requirements (stretcher, extra legroom, proximity to lavatory) |
| **Emergency procedures** | Any specific emergency procedures the crew should be aware of |
| **Physician details** | Name, qualifications, practice address, license number, signature, date, stamp |

> **Important:** The MEDIF Part 2 must be completed **no more than 10 days** before the departure date to ensure the medical assessment is current. Forms older than 10 days will be rejected.

### 2.4 MEDIF Submission Process

1. Passenger or agent requests the MEDIF form (available at airline.com/medical or from the Contact Centre).
2. Passenger completes Part 1 and has their physician complete Part 2.
3. Completed MEDIF is submitted to the airline's Medical Department via:
   - Email: medical@airline.com
   - Fax: +XX XXX XXX XXXX
   - Upload: airline.com/medical/upload
4. Medical Department reviews the form within **48–72 hours**.
5. Decision is communicated to the passenger and recorded in the PNR.
6. If approved, the SSR code MEDA is confirmed (status HK) with any conditions noted.

## 3. Conditions Requiring Medical Clearance

### 3.1 Mandatory Medical Clearance Required

The following conditions **always** require medical clearance before travel:

| Condition | Details | Clearance Requirements |
|-----------|---------|----------------------|
| **Recent surgery** | Any surgery performed **less than 10 days** before travel | MEDIF + fit-to-fly certificate. For abdominal/thoracic surgery, minimum 10 days post-op. For minor outpatient procedures, 48 hours may be sufficient with physician approval. |
| **Recent hospitalization** | Discharged from hospital **less than 7 days** before travel | MEDIF required. Must be medically stable. |
| **Communicable disease** | Active tuberculosis, chickenpox, measles, mumps, COVID-19 (if symptomatic), MRSA, or any notifiable disease | MEDIF + non-contagion certificate. Passenger may be denied boarding if contagion risk is assessed as high. |
| **Supplemental oxygen requirement** | Passenger requires oxygen during the flight | MEDIF + OXYG SSR. Only airline-approved portable oxygen concentrators (POCs) are permitted. See Section 5. |
| **Mental health crisis** | Passenger experiencing acute psychiatric episode, severe anxiety disorder, or any condition affecting ability to comply with safety instructions | MEDIF + psychiatric assessment. Medical escort may be required. |
| **Pregnancy >36 weeks** | Single pregnancy beyond 36 weeks gestation, or multiple pregnancy beyond 32 weeks | MEDIF + fit-to-fly certificate from obstetrician dated within 7 days of travel. See Section 3.2. |
| **Fractures with cast** | Plaster cast applied **less than 48 hours** before travel | MEDIF required. Cast must be bivalved (split) for flights >4 hours to allow for pressure changes. |
| **Deep vein thrombosis (DVT)** | Active or recent DVT (within 4 weeks) | MEDIF required. Anticoagulation therapy must be stable. |
| **Seizure disorder** | Uncontrolled epilepsy or seizure disorder | MEDIF required if seizure occurred within the last 24 hours. |
| **Severe anemia** | Hemoglobin below 7.5 g/dL | MEDIF required. May need supplemental oxygen. |
| **Pneumothorax** | Active or recent (within 6 weeks) | MEDIF required. Must have chest X-ray confirming resolution. |
| **Cardiac conditions** | Recent heart attack (<3 weeks), unstable angina, recent cardiac surgery (<2 weeks), uncontrolled heart failure | MEDIF required. Cardiac event within 3 weeks requires cardiologist clearance. |
| **Stroke / TIA** | Within 10 days | MEDIF required. Must be neurologically stable. |
| **Diving** | Scuba diving within **24 hours** before flight | Advisory only. No clearance required, but passenger should be informed of decompression risk. |

### 3.2 Pregnancy Travel Rules

| Gestation Period | Policy |
|-----------------|--------|
| **Up to 28 weeks** | No restrictions. No documentation required. |
| **28–36 weeks (single)** | Travel permitted with fit-to-fly certificate from physician/midwife dated within **7 days** of outbound travel and within **7 days** of return travel. |
| **28–32 weeks (multiple)** | Travel permitted with fit-to-fly certificate. |
| **36+ weeks (single)** | Travel **not permitted** unless medical clearance is granted for exceptional circumstances. |
| **32+ weeks (multiple)** | Travel **not permitted** unless medical clearance is granted for exceptional circumstances. |
| **Within 7 days post-delivery** | Travel not permitted. After 7 days, travel permitted with fit-to-fly certificate. |
| **Newborns** | Infants less than **48 hours old** are not accepted for travel. Infants 48 hours – 7 days require medical clearance. |

## 4. Fit-to-Fly Certificate

### 4.1 What is a Fit-to-Fly Certificate?

A fit-to-fly certificate (also known as a medical fitness declaration) is a document issued by a qualified medical practitioner confirming that a passenger is medically fit to undertake air travel.

### 4.2 Fit-to-Fly Certificate Format

The certificate must contain the following information:

| Required Element | Details |
|-----------------|---------|
| **Patient name** | Full name matching travel documents |
| **Date of birth** | DD/MM/YYYY |
| **Medical condition** | Diagnosis or reason for certificate |
| **Assessment date** | Date of medical examination |
| **Fitness statement** | Clear statement: "This patient is fit to travel by air on [date/date range]" |
| **Restrictions/conditions** | Any special requirements (e.g., "must travel with supplemental oxygen", "requires aisle seat") |
| **Physician details** | Full name, qualifications, medical license number |
| **Contact information** | Practice address, phone number, email |
| **Signature and stamp** | Physician's signature and official practice stamp |
| **Validity** | Must be dated **no more than 7 days** before travel |

### 4.3 Acceptable Certificate Issuers

- Licensed medical doctor (MD/DO)
- Registered specialist relevant to the condition
- Registered midwife (for pregnancy-related certificates only)
- Aviation medical examiner (AME)

> **Note:** Certificates from chiropractors, naturopaths, homeopaths, or other non-registered practitioners are **not accepted**.

## 5. Oxygen Equipment

### 5.1 Portable Oxygen Concentrators (POCs)

POCs are **approved** for use on board, subject to the following conditions:

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| **Approved models** | Must be FAA-approved. Current approved models include: Inogen One, Respironics SimplyGo, SeQual Eclipse, AirSep FreeStyle, DeVilbiss iGo. Full list available on airline.com/medical. |
| **Battery life** | Batteries must last for **150% of the planned flight time** (including potential delays). |
| **Spare batteries** | Passengers should carry at least **2 spare fully charged batteries** in carry-on baggage. |
| **Battery type** | Lithium-ion batteries only. Must not exceed 160 Wh per battery. Spare batteries must have terminals protected. |
| **Notification** | OXYG SSR must be added to the PNR at least **72 hours** before departure. |
| **Seating** | Passenger must be seated in a window seat or an aisle seat in a non-exit row. POC must fit under the seat in front. |
| **In-flight use** | Passenger is responsible for operating their own POC. Cabin crew will not operate medical equipment. |
| **Airline-provided POC** | Not available. Passengers must provide their own approved POC. |

### 5.2 Compressed Gas Cylinders

Compressed gas oxygen cylinders (tanks) are **NOT permitted** on board the aircraft, either in the cabin or in checked baggage.

| Item | Policy |
|------|--------|
| **Compressed oxygen cylinders** | Not accepted (carry-on or checked) |
| **Liquid oxygen containers** | Not accepted (carry-on or checked) |
| **Empty cylinders** | Accepted in checked baggage if valve is completely removed or the cylinder is verified completely empty |

### 5.3 Other Medical Equipment

| Equipment | Policy | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| **CPAP machine** | Allowed in cabin | Counts as a medical device, not against carry-on allowance. Must have sufficient battery. |
| **Nebulizer** | Allowed in cabin | Battery-operated only. No compressed gas cartridges. |
| **Insulin pump** | Allowed | Wear through security. Inform crew. |
| **EpiPen / auto-injectors** | Allowed in cabin | Must be in original packaging with prescription label. |
| **Syringes / needles** | Allowed in cabin | Requires a medical certificate or prescription. Sharps container for disposal. |
| **Wheelchair batteries (lithium)** | Allowed as checked | Max 300 Wh. Must be disconnected. Terminals insulated. Notify at check-in. |
| **Wheelchair batteries (spillable)** | Allowed as checked | Must remain upright. Terminals insulated. Packed to prevent short circuits. |
| **Hearing aids** | Allowed | Spare batteries in carry-on. |
| **Defibrillator (personal)** | Allowed in cabin | Must notify airline at booking. |

## 6. Stretcher Requests

### 6.1 Stretcher Requirements

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| **Booking** | Must be booked through the airline's Medical Department. Not available online. |
| **Deadline** | Minimum **72 hours** before departure (5 business days recommended for international flights). |
| **Seat requirement** | A stretcher installation requires **6 Economy seats** (2 rows of 3 seats) or the equivalent space. The passenger is charged for these seats. |
| **Pricing** | 6x the applicable Economy fare on the route + EUR 500 stretcher installation fee. |
| **Medical escort** | A qualified medical escort (doctor or nurse) **must** accompany the stretcher passenger. The escort occupies the adjacent seat. |
| **Aircraft limitation** | Stretchers can only be installed on wide-body aircraft. Not available on narrow-body or regional aircraft. |
| **Route limitation** | Subject to aircraft type on the route. Agent must verify equipment type before confirming. |
| **MEDIF requirement** | Full MEDIF (Parts 1 and 2) required. Must be cleared by the airline's Medical Department. |
| **SSR code** | STCR added to PNR upon confirmation. |
| **Ground handling** | Ambulift or ground-level boarding required. Coordinated by the Special Services desk. |
| **In-flight care** | All medical care during the flight is the responsibility of the accompanying medical escort. Cabin crew provide standard safety assistance only. |

### 6.2 Stretcher Cancellation

- Stretcher bookings cancelled **more than 72 hours** before departure: Full refund minus EUR 100 administrative fee.
- Stretcher bookings cancelled **less than 72 hours** before departure: 50% refund.
- No-show: No refund.

## 7. Medical Clearance Processing

### 7.1 Processing Timeline

| Step | Timeline |
|------|----------|
| MEDIF submission received | Day 0 |
| Initial review by medical coordinator | Within 24 hours |
| Specialist review (if required) | Day 1–2 |
| Decision communicated to passenger | Day 2–3 (48–72 hours from submission) |
| PNR updated with MEDA SSR status | Same day as decision |

### 7.2 Possible Outcomes

| Outcome | Description | Action |
|---------|-------------|--------|
| **APPROVED** | Passenger is cleared to fly without conditions | MEDA SSR confirmed (HK). No special requirements. |
| **APPROVED WITH CONDITIONS** | Passenger is cleared with specific requirements | MEDA SSR confirmed (HK). Conditions noted in PNR (e.g., oxygen, medical escort, specific seating). |
| **DEFERRED** | Additional information needed | Request further documentation from the passenger's physician. New processing timeline begins upon receipt. |
| **DENIED** | Passenger is not cleared to fly | MEDA SSR rejected (UN). Passenger informed with specific reasons. Right to appeal within 7 days. |

### 7.3 Appeal Process

If medical clearance is denied, the passenger may appeal by:
1. Providing additional medical documentation or a second opinion.
2. Submitting a formal appeal to the Chief Medical Officer at medical-appeal@airline.com.
3. Appeals are reviewed within **5 business days**.
4. The Chief Medical Officer's decision is final.

## 8. Medical Emergencies at the Airport

### 8.1 Pre-boarding Medical Event

If a passenger experiences a medical emergency at the airport before boarding:

1. Call airport medical services immediately.
2. Do **not** allow the passenger to board until assessed by medical personnel.
3. If the passenger is assessed as fit to fly, proceed with boarding.
4. If the passenger is assessed as unfit, deny boarding on medical grounds:
   - Rebook on the next available flight (no fee, treated as involuntary).
   - Arrange medical care at the airport.
   - Notify the passenger's emergency contact.
   - Document the event in the PNR and incident reporting system.

### 8.2 Denied Boarding on Medical Grounds

- The airline has the right to deny boarding if there is a reasonable belief that the passenger's condition:
  - Poses a safety risk to the passenger or others.
  - Could deteriorate during the flight.
  - Is a communicable disease posing a health risk to other passengers.
- This is an **involuntary denial** and EU261/2004 compensation does **not** apply (passenger-related cause).
- The airline must provide written confirmation of the denial reason upon the passenger's request.
